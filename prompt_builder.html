2 · Scope & purpose

"Find new companies" = Claude uses your starting point to discover additional candidates via NAICS codes. "Just enrich these" = Claude only researches the exact company names you typed — no new companies added.

15–30 per code tends to get the best-researched detail. Totals above ~50–75 in one prompt tend to come back thinner — batch it if you need more.

"Yes" broadens the search to suppliers, vendors, and related-but-differently-classified industries. "No" keeps it tightly scoped to your exact code(s).

The most important field — it tells Claude how to judge Category, Key Contact, Opportunity Fit, and Why This Fits for every company. Be specific.

Not a file upload — paste text directly (resume, CV, bio, LinkedIn summary, or just a few sentences — whatever you have). No length limit, but a tight paragraph of relevant highlights tends to work better than pasting a full multi-page document — it's easier for Claude to weigh correctly. Sharpens the Suggested Job Title Keywords column and gives Claude something real to reason from for Warm Introduction Path (former employers, overlapping experience) instead of guessing generically.

"Yes" (default) asks for the full formatted tracker: color-coded rating columns, a computed Suggested Priority Rank column, clickable Website links, autofilter, frozen panes, zebra striping, and separate Notes/Summary tabs — the same conventions used throughout this project, spelled out explicitly so you get them in any chat, not just this one. "No" asks for a plain data-only spreadsheet if you'd rather skip the extra formatting.

"Yes" (default) adds a second sheet with one-click LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, and Google Jobs search links per company — built from the Company and Suggested Job Title Keywords columns using Excel HYPERLINK() formulas, so the links stay live and update automatically if you edit either cell later. This is a search-link generator, not a source of actual live postings — for real postings data, that's a separate tool/step (see the User Guide).

"Yes" (default) adds a tab of real, upcoming industry conferences and trade shows relevant to your stated industry/purpose — one row per event, not per company, since the same event is often relevant to several companies on your list at once. Each event's "Relevant To" field reuses your Category column, so it stays connected to however you've grouped your companies.

Different from the tab above — this one is one row per company (not per event), tracking each company's own public activity: recent LinkedIn posts/announcements, podcast or webcast appearances, and upcoming events/tradeshows they're attending or speaking at. This is much harder to verify reliably via search than a public conference calendar, so expect real gaps — Claude is instructed to leave rows blank rather than invent activity that isn't actually findable.

Step 2 (optional) — LinkedIn Contact Enrichment

This is a separate, focused prompt for cross-referencing your LinkedIn Contacts export against your Top Targeted Companies to surface warm introduction paths. This is a heavier processing lift, so it works best with 25 or fewer companies — the more companies you list, the more Claude has to fuzzy-match against for every single contact, which slows down the processing and can hurt match quality.

Run it separately from the Target Company Prompt Builder above: run that tool first, then paste your Top 25 companies below to build this prompt. Download the .txt, then paste that prompt into Claude along with your exported LinkedIn Contacts .csv export. There's no need to upload or paste the CSV here — this tool never touches the actual file.

NOTE:

Steps to export your LinkedIn profile (w/ contact data):

  • Log into desktop LinkedIn.
  • Click your Me icon at the top of the page.
  • Select Settings & Privacy.
  • Click Data privacy on the left menu.
  • Click Get a copy of your data.
  • Choose Download larger data archive, including connections, verifications, contacts, account history, and information we infer about you based on your profile and activity.

For each match found, the report has:

  • Company — from your list above
  • Matched Contact Name
  • Contact's Title — as listed in the CSV
  • Contact's LinkedIn Profile URL — if present in the CSV
  • Current or Former — notes if the CSV or context suggests this is a past employer for that contact; a former employee can still be a useful warm intro, but it's a different kind of connection than someone there now, so these aren't blended together
  • Match Confidence — High (exact company name match), Medium (clear variant/subsidiary match), or Low (plausible but uncertain, e.g. a similar name or ambiguous abbreviation)

If a target company has no matching contact in your CSV, it's still included in the results as "No match found" rather than omitted, so you can see coverage across your full list. The result is formatted as an Excel workbook (.xlsx).
